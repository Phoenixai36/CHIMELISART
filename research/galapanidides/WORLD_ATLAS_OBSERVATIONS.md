# GALAPANÍDIDES — World Atlas / Visual Observations v0.1

## Evidence basis

This first pass combines the user's recorded physical-book pages with the public Chimelis Art gallery as secondary visual context. The physical pencil edition remains the primary source for Galapanídides.

## Observed visual grammar

- Hand-drawn graphite/pencil linework with strong variation in density.
- Human and humanoid faces/figures appear as major compositional anchors.
- Organic, tubular and serpentine structures recur around or through figures.
- Eyes and facial apertures are visually salient motifs.
- Geometric structures coexist with organic anatomy rather than being separated into different visual systems.
- Large compositions favor vertical depth, central focal masses and nested forms over conventional comic panels.
- The page can function as a complete visual field rather than merely an illustration accompanying text.
- The visual language supports ambiguity between anatomy, architecture, organism and symbol.

## Public gallery context (secondary)

The current portfolio includes works titled “Dos Mundos”, “Nada es lo que era”, “Transformación Paralela”, “Jeroglífico”, “Futuro Incierto”, “Evolución”, “Equilibrio” and “Asimetría”. These titles are useful as context for a broader artistic vocabulary around transformation, parallelism, asymmetry and symbolic imagery, but they are **not treated as evidence of Galapanídides lore** without cross-checking against the book.

## World-building consequences

For the first Unity render pass, model the environment as a field of nested visual relationships rather than as a conventional room or landscape.

### Layer proposal

1. **Paper / void substrate** — neutral spatial field carrying the visual memory of the page.
2. **Deep forms** — slow, large-scale organic/geometric masses.
3. **Architectural-organic forms** — intermediate structures that can read simultaneously as mechanism, organism or symbolic construction.
4. **Figure anchors** — silhouettes/facial forms used for scale and orientation.
5. **Foreground fragments** — partial occlusions that create depth and make the composition traversable.
6. **Dimensional field** — subtle distortion, parallax and/or mask transitions reserved for moments where the visual composition implies a boundary.
