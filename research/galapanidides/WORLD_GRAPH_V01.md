## Strategic state

The story creates an asymmetric network:

- Galapanídides have superior traversal/embodiment technology.
- The Matèria Fosca destroys portals and constrains routes.
- Some Matèria Fosca warriors can pass as human.
- Human society is largely unaware of the conflict.
- Access to worlds is therefore more important than simple physical territory.

## Tossa de Mar node

The text's explicit Tossa de Mar reference should become a special world node, but not yet a fully designed level. The exact physical relationship between the buried spacecraft, the medieval wall and the imperceptible route must be re-photographed and transcribed at higher resolution before detailed geography is declared canonical.

## Game-world consequence

The level map should eventually behave as a **portal graph**, not a conventional sequence of disconnected stages.

A player action that damages, repairs, discovers or changes a portal can alter which realities are reachable. This follows directly from the narrative's emphasis on portals as scarce infrastructure.

## Reality-state design

A location may have multiple versions:

```text
LOCATION_X
├── HUMAN_REALITY
├── GALAPANÍDIDES_ECHO
├── MATÈRIA_FOSCA_STATE
└── TEMPORAL_VARIANTS
```

These variants are an adaptation system derived from the book's explicit multi-reality and time-travel rules; they are not presented as named canon locations.
