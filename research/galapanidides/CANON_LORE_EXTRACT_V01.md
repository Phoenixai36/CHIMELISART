# GALAPANÍDIDES — Canon Lore Extract v0.1

> Evidence-first extraction from the user's five page-turning videos of the physical pencil edition. This file distinguishes what the photographed text explicitly states from interpretation. It does not treat the novel's claims about real-world events as factual history.

## 1. Origin and nature of the Galapanídides

The story places the civilization in a remote location at the infinite edge of the universe. It describes an unimaginably advanced civilization whose beings possess exceptional intelligence and knowledge.

Their technology allows them to decompose their cells, make themselves practically invisible, move those cells to the past, present or future, and recompose themselves in whatever form they desire. They possess gigantic spacecraft capable of crossing universes and parallel worlds across infinite distances.

Their technology and mode of existence are presented as the envy of other universes.

## 2. Planet Galapanídides

The place is explicitly identified as the planet Galapanídides. Its inhabitants are practically immortal: they can live indefinitely provided their organism is not attacked destructively.

They live eternally and harmoniously until destructive beings appear. These beings annihilate and devastate everything in their path. They are explicitly named **Matèria Fosca / Materia Oscura** in the narrative.

Most inhabitants of the idyllic planet are killed horribly. A surviving group devises a plan to abandon the planet and begin again elsewhere.

## 3. The exodus

The surviving group decides to escape certain death. They fuse their minds into a single entity and travel for years in sophisticated spacecraft through unknown universes.

The escape is difficult because the warriors of the Matèria Fosca pursue them. The Galapanídides' abilities and technology allow them to traverse portals into universes with different realities.

This establishes a major world rule: identity, embodiment, time and location are mutable rather than fixed.

## 4. Arrival in the human world

The Galapanídides eventually enter the human world. The narrative places their activity in relation to Earth and human civilization.

A group of Galapanídides creates **rastrejadors** (trackers/scouts) capable of changing form and size. They enter Earth's atmosphere and examine changes occurring in both worlds. The story links these scouts to unidentified objects appearing on Earth at different times.

The text explicitly references reports attributed to the U.S. Department of Defense/Pentagon concerning unidentified aerial phenomena. These references are part of the novel's fictional narrative and should not be represented as evidence that the story's extraterrestrial explanation is real.

## 5. Portals and dimensional warfare

The story repeatedly establishes portals as the infrastructure connecting worlds. Moving from one world to another is difficult.

The Matèria Fosca damages/destroys portals, leaving only limited spaces through which infiltration can occur. At one point the Galapanídides are trapped in another universe and cannot access the humans, while the Matèria Fosca can still access humanity.

This creates an asymmetric strategic situation: the Galapanídides have superior technology but increasingly constrained access routes.

## 6. Human infiltration

The narrative states that some warriors of the Matèria Fosca have adopted human appearance. It becomes impossible to distinguish a Matèria Fosca being from a human being by appearance alone.

Some infiltrate high spheres of power, explicitly naming bankers, judges and politicians. The text frames this as a new strategy of the Matèria Fosca and as a threat to humanity.

This is a major mechanic/lore opportunity: **identity cannot be solved reliably by appearance**.

## 7. The biological/viral attack

The novel connects a Matèria Fosca strategy with a virus and with the COVID-19 pandemic, including a statement about a first portal being crossed in China and deaths by May 2022. These are narrative claims made by the book, not verified causal explanations of COVID-19.

For game adaptation, this material should be treated as fictional lore. Do not present it as real-world medical or historical fact.

The later text states that Matèria Fosca warriors/virus carriers had suffered losses but continued trying to infect humanity, then changed strategy and produced different versions of the same virus whose effects were no longer the same.

## 8. Earth-side human ignorance

The story states that ordinary people continued their daily lives unaware of what was happening around them. This contrasts the hidden dimensional conflict with mundane human life.

The Galapanídides remain highly alert. The narrative describes the period as delicate for both humans and Galapanídides.

## 9. Tossa de Mar / Catalonia anchor

A particularly important geographical passage states that one Galapanídides spacecraft became buried near a small place identified in the text as **Tossa de Mar**, on the Costa Brava in Catalonia, associated with a large medieval wall. The passage then describes the same coordinates as significant to an imperceptible passage/route and to possible access by the Matèria Fosca.

This should be treated as an explicit in-universe Earth location until the exact page wording is re-photographed at higher resolution.

## 10. Temporal / spatial end state

The text states that the future became uncertain/invisible and that the Matèria Fosca had neutralized some forms of space-time.

At that point the Galapanídides are trapped in another universe and cannot access the humans. The Matèria Fosca, however, has accessed humanity, so humanity is again endangered.

## 11. Strongest adaptation rules

These are direct consequences of the photographed text, not invented lore:

- Bodies are transformable.
- Cellular decomposition/recomposition is possible.
- Time travel is possible through the civilization's technology.
- Parallel universes are traversable.
- Portals are strategic infrastructure.
- Portals can be damaged or destroyed.
- The Matèria Fosca can infiltrate through remaining routes.
- Galapanídides can merge minds into a single entity.
- Scouts can change form and size.
- Matèria Fosca warriors can appear human.
- Human institutions can be infiltrated.
- Earth contains hidden dimensional access points.
- Tossa de Mar is a named Earth-side location in the narrative.
- Humanity is largely unaware of the conflict.

## 12. Adaptation boundary

Do not add named protagonists, exact factions, technologies, objectives, maps or mechanics as canon unless they can be traced to a page in the corpus or are explicitly marked as an adaptation invention.

The game may build systems from these rules, but the source text remains authoritative for the original story.
